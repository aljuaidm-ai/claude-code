Player language
Option A

Club Felt

A traditional card room: green felt, a wooden rail and gold trim. It feels like the table at home or at the club.

Suits: players who learned at a real table and want it to feel familiar. It's the safest choice for broad appeal.

Risk: many card apps look like this, so the brand has to stand out through other details.

Option B

Retro '99

A nod to Yahoo! Games: gray beveled buttons, a blue title bar and Verdana. It's pure nostalgia for players who were there.

Suits: players who remember Yahoo! Games. It works best as an optional unlockable skin.

Risk: it looks dated to new or younger players if it's the default.

Option C

Arena Night

A modern competitive look: a deep navy table with glowing highlights, rank badges and pill buttons. It feels like a ranked match.

Suits: ladders, seasons and tournaments. It fits the "arena" name and appeals to younger players.

Risk: it's less warm, and some traditional players may find it cold.

Option D

Salon

An elegant parlor: burgundy patterned cloth, a brass rail and teal card backs. It's warm and a little luxurious, and it suits Spanish, German and Arabic players alike.

Suits: a premium, social feel. It sets the brand apart from the green-felt apps.

Risk: the pattern must stay subtle so cards stay easy to read on small phones.

My recommendation

Mix A or D with C

Make the table warm and traditional (Club Felt or Salon), because that's where players spend hours. Use the modern Arena style for the lobby, rankings, seasons and tournaments, where it should feel competitive. Offer Retro '99 and the other table looks as free or earned skins, so each player can pick their own. That choice affects only their own screen, just like language.

Your language idea

Every player sees the game in their own language. I agree, and it's the right call.

At one table, Layla plays in Arabic, Marisol in Spanish, Klaus in German and you in English. The server never sends text. It sends events such as "Klaus took the pile, 14 cards", and each device writes them out in that player's language. Here is the same moment on four screens:

  1. All rule and game text is translated: menus, rules, hints, the scorepad, the move log and tutorials. The server sends codes, never sentences.
  2. Quick chat is how players talk across languages. Phrases like "Nice play!", "May I go out?" and "Good game" are sent as codes, so each player reads them in their own language. This matters most for 2v2 partners who don't share a language.
  3. Free-text chat appears as typed, with a "Translate" button (automatic translation, with the original always shown). Lobby rooms per language (English, Español, Deutsch, العربية) plus one International room.
  4. Arabic mirrors the screen right to left and uses the Cairo font. Players can choose Western (123) or Arabic-Indic (١٢٣) digits. Card corners always show A K Q J so every player sees the same cards.
  5. Canasta vocabulary is kept per language. A natural canasta is "limpia" (Spanish), "rein" (German) and "نظيفة" (Arabic). Native Canasta players in each language will review the translations before launch.
  6. Matchmaking isn't split by language, so the player pool stays large. Players can set "prefer my language" for Quick Match.
